# Expert interview guide

The purpose of these sessions is to **invalidate assumptions quickly**, not sell OpenPaw.

## Who

Prioritize people who routinely see:

- reactive / fearful dogs;
- behavior cases with medical contributors;
- owners struggling to describe what happened between visits;
- longitudinal cases where recovery, medication, sleep, pain, or context matter.

Record credentials and role so feedback is interpretable.

## Session structure

### 1. Current workflow

Before showing OpenPaw:

- What information do owners usually bring you?
- What information do you wish they had?
- Which owner observations are most useful?
- Which are commonly misleading?
- How do you currently measure progress?
- What makes you change a plan or ask for veterinary workup?
- How much longitudinal data is actually reviewable in practice?

### 2. Show the pretotype

Do not explain the intended conclusion.

Ask the expert to narrate what they see in:

- baseline;
- timeline;
- one reaction episode;
- recovery;
- expert annotation view.

### 3. Probe trust

For each derived insight:

- Would you use this?
- What evidence would you need underneath it?
- What could cause a false interpretation?
- Would a categorical state be safer than a score?
- Which signals should never be collapsed together?
- What language would you consider misleading or clinically inappropriate?

### 4. Probe actionability

- What would this make you ask the owner?
- Could this alter management, referral, testing, or follow-up?
- Which view belongs to the owner vs veterinarian vs behavior professional?
- What is the smallest amount of data that creates value?
- When would an alert be useful? When would it become noise?

### 5. Hardware requirements

Only after validating the information need:

- Which signals matter enough to justify sensing hardware?
- Required sampling frequency?
- Tolerable missing data?
- Collar placement / fit constraints?
- Would you trust owner-built hardware?
- What validation against clinical equipment would you require?

### 6. Real-time assistance

For the later glasses concept:

- Which body-language cues are teachable in real time?
- Which cues are too ambiguous for automated prompting?
- What interventions could safely be pre-authored by the dog's professional?
- How minimal must a cue be during a walk?
- What should the system explicitly refuse to infer?

## Capture format

For every important statement, record:

- participant role / credential;
- exact problem or decision;
- current workaround;
- proposed OpenPaw surface;
- usefulness;
- trust requirement;
- risk / failure mode;
- requested change;
- confidence in our interpretation.

Preserve direct credit for expert-originated ideas in design notes and implementation issues.

## Strong evidence of demand

Prefer behavioral evidence over compliments:

- asks to try it on a current case;
- volunteers sample data;
- requests a specific workflow/export;
- introduces another professional;
- says it would change a real follow-up or decision;
- is willing to review another iteration.

"This is cool" is not validation.

## Safety framing

OpenPaw should never pressure an expert to endorse an automated diagnosis. Ask professionals to identify where the boundary between **observation**, **decision support**, and **clinical/behavioral judgment** should sit.
