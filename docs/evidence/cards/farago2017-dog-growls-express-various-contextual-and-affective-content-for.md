population: "40 adult human listeners (14 men, 26 women); 10 s growl bouts recorded from 18 family dogs in three contexts (food guarding, threatening stranger, tug-of-war play), eight samples per context"
measures: "Listeners rated each bout on inner-state scales (aggression, fear, despair, happiness, playfulness) and picked its context out of three"
main_finding: "Listeners named the right context for 63% of growl bouts against a 33% chance level; play bouts were recognised 81% of the time, food guarding 60% and threatening 50%, and threatening and food-guarding growls were often confused with each other. Women and people experienced with dogs scored higher. The authors note earlier work in which listeners could not tell single playful and threatening growls apart."
effect_direction: "Growl bouts carry context information for human listeners, above chance but with frequent errors"
limitations: "Small listener sample from one lab; recorded growls in three staged contexts played without any picture of the dog; one in three bouts was still assigned the wrong context, so sound alone is not a reliable read."
